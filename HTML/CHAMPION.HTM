Match observations:

USWA Championship Wrestling | July 6th, 1991
Memphis, TN | Venue: Mid South Coliseum
Attendance: 4,500 AVG: Ticket $12.00
Gate: $54,000.00, Broadcast: Live TV.

The Red, White and Bruised Tour rolled into the Mid South Coliseum on
Saturday night, and 4,500 fans saw Andre the Giant do what nobody else has
done all summer. He put Kerry Von Erich to sleep.

Kerry asked for this match, and he got everything he asked for. For most of
fourteen minutes he went at the giant with everything he had, punches,
kicks, even a try at the slam that nobody thought he could get. But Andre
caught him coming in, locked his arms around Kerry's back and squeezed.
Kerry refused to give up. The referee lifted his arm three times, and three
times it fell. At 14:43 the bell rang, and Paul Ellering walked his giant
back up the aisle with a grin on his face.

What happened next has all of Memphis talking. As officials tended to
Kerry, Ric Flair walked down the aisle in a suit with the Big Gold belt
over his shoulder. The Nature Boy waved off the officials, knelt beside
Kerry and helped him sit up. Then he leaned down and said something only
Kerry could hear, stood up, straightened his tie and walked out without a
word. Five days ago Flair cost Kevin Von Erich his match with Terry Funk.
Now he's helping Kerry off the mat. What does Ric Flair want with the Von
Erichs?

Earlier, Paul Ellering told Dave Brown that Kerry was a washed-up pretty
boy with a famous last name. He also said Toshiaki Kawada's decision to
turn him down and go home to Japan was Kawada's loss, not his. After
tonight, it's hard to argue with Ellering about Kerry.

Scott Steiner made it official. With Eddie Marlin in the ring, Steiner
handed one of the tag team title belts to Tully Blanchard, and Marlin
announced Steiner and Blanchard as the USWA Tag Team Champions. Not
everyone was happy. Robert Fuller, who stood by Steiner when his brother
left, got in Blanchard's face and said the belt should have been his.
Blanchard told him that's how the business works. Steiner stepped between
them, and Fuller walked out. Stan Hansen and Dan Spivey want those belts
back, and it looks like the champions have a problem in their own corner
too.

Diamond Studd had his first match in the USWA, and Dennis Condrey of Hot
Stuff International never had a chance. Studd won with the Final Reckoning
at 7:04, then stood in the corner and tapped his waist where a championship
belt would be. Backstage, Missy Hyatt and Steve Austin tried to bring Studd
into Hot Stuff International. Studd told them the only thing he wants from
Austin is the Southern Championship, and he'll see him in Louisville next
Saturday. Austin didn't like how friendly Missy was with Studd, and he let
her know it.

The ugliest moment of the night happened backstage. Jamie Dundee told Corey
Maclin his father picked a washed-up legend over his own blood, and that
Paul Ellering is the only man who ever believed in him. When Jimmy Valiant
tried to calm him down, Jamie pulled a chain and beat Valiant over the head
with it. Jamie walked off with a message: tell my father that one's for
him. Bill Dundee defends the Television Championship in Evansville on
Wednesday night, and nobody knows what frame of mind he'll be in.

Sam Houston opened the show and kept the edge he showed in Jonesboro. He
beat Johnny Rich with a tiger suplex at 6:14. Then Scotty Flamingo came out
and called him a washed-up rodeo act, a walking Marlboro ad with no
ratings. Houston went after him, and Flamingo ran.

In six-man action, Scott Taylor and the New Kids beat El Hijo Del Santo,
The Spellbinder and The Masked Avenger when Taylor pinned the Avenger with
the Fall of the Angels at 8:24. Santo and Taylor shook hands again after
the match.

Next Saturday night the tour hits Louisville Gardens. Kerry Von Erich and
Bret Hart team up against Andre and Jamie Dundee, and Steve Austin finally
meets Diamond Studd.
