Match observations:

USWA Worldwide | July 17th, 1991
Jonesboro, AR | Venue: Earl Bell Community Center
Attendance: 700 AVG: Ticket $5.00
Gate: $3,500.00, Broadcast: Syndicated TV Taping.

USWA Worldwide returned to the Earl Bell Community Center, and 700 fans
in Jonesboro saw blood, a crack in Hot Stuff and the best wrestling match
on television in weeks. The show earned three stars overall.

In the main event, Mark Canterbury finally got Moondog Spot alone in the
ring, and for nearly fourteen minutes he made the man who turned on him
at Star Wars pay. Then the Honky Tonk Man interfered, and the referee
threw the match out. Canterbury won by disqualification, but the beating
was only getting started. Honky, Spot and Moondog Rex went three on one,
and Rex cracked Canterbury across the forehead with the dog bone. Honky
had his guitar raised over a kneeling, bleeding Canterbury when Robert
Fuller came sprinting through the curtain and cleared the ring. Canterbury
pulled himself up, his face covered in blood, and waved all three of them
back in. Nobody came. Two stars.

The match of the night belonged to Eddie Guerrero and the Spellbinder,
eleven minutes of non-stop action that ended when Guerrero locked in the
camel clutch and the Spellbinder had to submit. Four and a quarter stars.
Guerrero has been the best young wrestler on every card he's worked.

Scotty Flamingo opened the show and made it three straight in the
Flamingo Invitational Series. Jim Powers pushed him all the way, but when
Flamingo hit a belly-to-belly suplex at 7:03, he put his feet on the
ropes for the pin, and the referee never saw it. Three and a half stars.
Sam Houston walked out and asked for the next invitation. Flamingo told
him invitations go to people who matter, called him a rodeo clown, and
ran up the aisle before Houston could reach him.

Brickhouse Brown pinned Dennis Condrey with a lariat at 10:09, three
stars, and Condrey's bad night got worse after the bell. Doug Gilbert
stormed around the ring and got in his face for losing to the man the
Gilberts face Saturday. Condrey fired back, Doug shoved him, and Missy
Hyatt had to jump between them. Condrey walked to the back alone.

The Memphis Mafia made quick work of Les Thornton and Lino Ingram when
Rex King forced Ingram to submit to a leglock in 3:49. Afterward Dirty
White Boy and King told Stan Hansen and Dan Spivey that the next time
they come through the ropes in a Mafia match, they deal with the Mafia
first.

Jerry Lawler told Lance Russell that Bret Hart only teamed with Kerry Von
Erich in Louisville to study him up close before their Iron Man match,
and that Kerry stealing the pin proved he's desperate. "A man who's
scared does," the King said. Jimmy Valiant, his head still bandaged from
Jamie Dundee's chain, accepted Jamie's challenge for a no-DQ match in
Dallas, with Bill Dundee promising to be at ringside.

Next stop: the Sportatorium in Dallas on Saturday. Ric Flair vs. Terry
Funk, Valiant vs. Jamie Dundee with no DQ, the Gilberts vs. Diamond Studd
and Brickhouse Brown, and Kerry Von Erich and Bret Hart face to face.
