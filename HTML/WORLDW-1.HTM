Match observations:

USWA Worldwide | July 3rd, 1991
Jonesboro, AR | Venue: Earl Bell Community Center
Attendance: 600 AVG: Ticket $5.00
Gate: $3,000.00, Broadcast: Syndicated TV Taping.

The Red, White and Bruised Tour kicked off in front of a loud crowd at the
Earl Bell Community Center, and two nights after Star Wars the fans in
Jonesboro made it clear they wanted more. They got it. Nearly every story
coming out of the Mid South Coliseum moved forward on Wednesday night.

The Boogie Woogie Man is back, and he's winning. Jimmy Valiant teamed with
Bill Dundee against Dirty White Boy and Rex King of the Memphis Mafia in
the main event. The Mafia worked over Valiant for long stretches, and Rex
King thought he had the momentum, but Valiant caught him coming in and
locked on the sleeperhold. King fought it as long as he could before he had
to give it up at 10:22. After what Andre the Giant did to him at Star Wars,
nobody needed that win more than Jimmy Valiant.

Eddie Guerrero and Rob Zakowski may have stolen the show. Zakowski matched
Guerrero hold for hold for more than ten minutes, and the crowd was on its
feet for every near fall. Guerrero finally went to the top rope and came
down with the frog splash at 11:11. Zakowski earned a lot of respect in
defeat.

Terry Funk made headlines before a bell ever rang. Funk walked out with
Chris Adams, Eric Embry, Tom Prichard and Kevin Von Erich and announced
that the Texas Bunch is no more. They are now the Texas Outlaws. Kevin said
he gave Funk his word at Star Wars and he'll keep it, but he also said
Terry didn't beat him on Monday night. Ric Flair did. Funk agreed, and he
had a message for the Nature Boy: if Flair wants to play games with his
family, he'll have to deal with all of them.

The Outlaws didn't waste any time. Later in the night, Chris Adams welcomed
newcomer Jim Powers to the USWA. Powers came out swinging and showed plenty
of power, but Adams caught him with the superkick at 5:27. Adams challenges
Bill Dundee for the Television Title next Wednesday in Evansville.

Fans who missed Star Wars saw the footage of Ric Flair walking to ringside
and costing Kevin Von Erich his match. The Nature Boy still has the Big
Gold belt, and Eddie Marlin has confirmed Flair is under contract with the
USWA. What Flair wants is anybody's guess.

Mark Canterbury is done being a manager. In footage from Star Wars, a
bitter Canterbury said he pulled the Moondogs out of the gutter, and they
repaid him with a chair to the back and a handshake with the Honky Tonk
Man. He promised to send the dogs back to the pound and to smash Honky's
guitar over his head. Honky didn't seem worried. He needed only 2:24 to put
away Oliver Peters with the Shake, Rattle and Roll.

The New Kids and Scott Taylor kept their momentum going against Jan Vang,
Jody Burt and Johnny Rich. Tony Williams came off the top with a flying
cross body on Rich for the win at 6:15.

Sam Houston opened the show, and this wasn't the Sam Houston the fans know.
There was no smile and no wave of the hat. Houston said he's been
overlooked in this company for too long, and he blamed loudmouths like
Scotty Flamingo for poisoning the locker room. He said from now on he's
taking what's his. Scotty Flamingo, consider yourself warned.

Kerry Von Erich was honest backstage. He said he shook Bret Hart's hand at
Star Wars, but the loss is still eating at him. Then Eddie Marlin walked in
with the chance Kerry asked for. This Saturday night, live from the Mid
South Coliseum, Kerry Von Erich will face Andre the Giant in the main
event.

The tour moves to Memphis on Saturday. Kerry faces Andre. Diamond Studd,
the man who cleared out Hot Stuff International at Star Wars, has his first
USWA match against Dennis Condrey. Then it's Evansville on July 10 and
Louisville Gardens on July 13, where Kerry and Bret Hart join forces
against Andre and Jamie Dundee and Steve Austin finally gets his hands on
Diamond Studd.
